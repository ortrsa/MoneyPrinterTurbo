Basenjis do not bark. The shape of their larynx is different, so instead of barking, they yodel. Owners call the sound a baroo. Use the plain word "yodel" or "yodels" -- do not invent variations of it, and keep "baroo" as its own short sentence.
Every dog's nose print is unique, with its own pattern of ridges and creases, much like a human fingerprint. The Canadian Kennel Club actually accepted nose prints as official dog identification for decades. Phrase this in the PAST tense -- they accepted them from 1938 until 1992 and then switched to microchips, so do NOT say kennel clubs use nose prints today.
Dalmatians are born completely white. Their spots only start appearing after about two weeks, and keep developing for months afterwards.
Newfoundlands have webbed feet and a water-resistant coat, and were bred specifically to haul drowning people out of the sea. Some are still trained as water rescue dogs today.
Greyhounds can hit around 45 miles an hour, making them the fastest dogs on Earth and, over a short distance, faster than a racehorse.
