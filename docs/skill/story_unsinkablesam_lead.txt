2	Solidly documented (multiple independent sources, contemporary 1941 press coverage, undisputed): a black-and-white cat named Oscar was rescued when the aircraft carrier HMS Ark Royal sank on 14 November 1941 after a U-boat torpedo strike near Gibraltar. The crew nicknamed him "Unsinkable Sam." Photographs of the rescue were published in British newspapers at the time. He retired to the offices of the Governor of Gibraltar, was later adopted by a seaman and moved to a home for sailors in Belfast, and died there in 1955. A pastel portrait of him by Georgina Shaw-Baker is held by the National Maritime Museum, Greenwich -- described by Royal Museums Greenwich as the only undisputed depiction of him.

3	HEDGED, not independently confirmed: the popular version of the story claims the same cat was earlier aboard the German battleship Bismarck (sunk 27 May 1941, ~2,200 crew, only 115 survivors) and was fished from the water by the British destroyer HMS Cossack -- which was itself torpedoed and sunk in October 1941, after which the cat was transferred to the Ark Royal. This is the part of the story that gives it its "three ships" hook, but: (a) Ludovic Kennedy's detailed contemporary account of the Bismarck's sinking does not mention a cat at all; (b) the claim traces to sailors' own telling rather than independent documentation; (c) at least one investigation (War History Online) found conflicting photographs -- one commonly shared as "Unsinkable Sam" is more likely a different WWII ship's cat, Simon of HMS Amethyst. Scripted accordingly: the Bismarck/Cossack legs are explicitly attributed to "the sailors' own telling," and the Ark Royal leg is explicitly flagged as the one part every historian agrees is real.
