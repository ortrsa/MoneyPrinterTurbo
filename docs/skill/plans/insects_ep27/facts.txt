Fire ants link their bodies into a living raft during floods, keeping the whole colony afloat for weeks at a time.
Honeybees dance to share directions: the angle points to the food, the wiggle's length says how far.
A bombardier beetle mixes two chemicals inside its own body and fires a spray close to boiling point at anything that threatens it.
A praying mantis has just one ear, tuned to detect bats, and it's the only insect that can turn its head almost all the way around.
A cockroach can live about a week without its head. It breathes through holes in its body, and only dies of thirst.
And the deadliest hunter alive isn't a lion or a shark. It's the dragonfly, landing a kill up to ninety five percent of the time it tries.
