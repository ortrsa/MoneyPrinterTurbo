SOURCES CHECKED:
- NOAA Fisheries / sharks4kids: sandbar sharks grow through roughly 35,000
  teeth over a lifetime, teeth constantly replaced in rows.
- Multiple sources: shark skeletons are entirely cartilage, no bones.
- Frontiers in Marine Science (Feb 2021) / Nature / National Geographic:
  the kitefin shark (Dalatias licha) confirmed as the largest known
  bioluminescent vertebrate, glowing blue-green from its belly and fins,
  hormone-regulated (not nerve-regulated like most bioluminescent life).
- A-Z Animals / Slate / Snopes / PETA: global shark deaths average 5-15
  people per year; vending machines (~2/yr), cows (~22/yr US), and
  lightning (~20/yr US) each kill more people annually than sharks do
  worldwide -- widely cross-verified, not a single-source claim.
- ScienceDaily / Science.org / Britannica (2016 radiocarbon-dating study):
  Greenland sharks are the longest-lived vertebrates known, life
  expectancy of at least 272 years, up to ~392 (+/-120), and they don't
  reach sexual maturity until roughly age 150.
- Forbes / Natural History Museum / Smithsonian: sharks first appear in
  the fossil record ~400-450 million years ago; the earliest known trees
  appear ~385 million years ago -- sharks predate trees by at least
  ~50-65 million years depending on source. Scripted as "before trees
  did," not with a precise year-gap number, since sources vary on the
  exact margin.

VERIFIED FACTS:
- ~35,000 teeth over a sandbar shark's lifetime, constant replacement.
- No bones -- cartilage skeleton (true of all sharks, not just this one).
- Kitefin shark: largest known bioluminescent vertebrate, confirmed 2021.
- Sharks kill far fewer people per year than several ordinary causes
  (vending machines, cows, lightning) -- genuine myth-correction.
- Greenland sharks: longest-lived vertebrate, 400+ year lifespan, ~150
  years to sexual maturity.
- Sharks predate trees in the fossil record by tens of millions of years,
  and have survived multiple mass extinctions (hedged as "multiple," not
  a specific count, since the exact number sharks lived through depends
  on precisely when in the fossil record their origin is dated).
