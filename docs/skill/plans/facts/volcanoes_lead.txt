VERIFIED FACTS:
- Kawah Ijen, Indonesia: the "blue lava" seen in famous night photos is not
  lava at all -- it's sulfuric gas igniting on contact with air above
  ~360C, burning blue. Only visible at night; in daylight the volcano
  looks ordinary. Genuine myth-correction, not a hedge.
- ~75% is the most commonly cited figure across sources for the share of
  Earth's total volcanic activity/magma output happening underwater along
  the ~65,000km mid-ocean ridge system (estimates range 75-90% depending
  on measurement method -- used the most-cited figure).
- Volcanic lightning ("dirty thunderstorms"): ash, rock fragments and ice
  colliding within an eruption plume generate static charge via
  triboelectric charging, the same basic mechanism as rubbing a balloon,
  building an electric field that triggers real lightning.
- Yellowstone: a supervolcano; its most recent caldera-forming eruption was
  ~640,000 years ago and is estimated at ~2,500x the volume of the 1980
  Mount St. Helens eruption. Not implying it's imminent -- just the scale
  of the last one.
- Olympus Mons (Mars): ~21km high above the Martian datum (~2.5x Mount
  Everest's height above sea level), a shield volcano with a footprint
  roughly the size of Arizona/Poland. Confirmed as the largest volcano in
  the solar system, no close competitor.
- Io (moon of Jupiter): the most volcanically active body in the solar
  system. Hundreds of active volcanoes; the tallest documented sulfur
  plume reached roughly 500km above the surface. Driven by tidal flexing
  from Jupiter's gravity (Io's solid body rises and falls ~100m per
  orbit), not internal radioactive decay like Earth.
