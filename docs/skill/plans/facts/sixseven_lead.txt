   satanic cult. Corrected to match.

VERIFIED FACTS:
- "Doot Doot (6 7)" by rapper Skrilla, officially released via Priority
  Records on 3 Feb 2025 (an unofficial version had leaked online in Dec
  2024). Hook line: "6-7, I just bipped right on the highway." Skrilla has
  said on record he never assigned the phrase a meaning and doesn't intend
  to; competing folk theories include Chicago's 67th Street and the police
  radio code "10-67" (used in some departments for a death).
- The phrase spread through basketball edit culture, especially clips of
  NBA player LaMelo Ball, listed at exactly 6'7" (2.01m). Overtime Elite
  player Taylen "TK" Kinney is credited with popularizing the accompanying
  hand gesture (palms up, alternating lift).
- 31 March 2025: basketball YouTuber Cam Wilder posted a video (~13:24 mark)
  showing a boy, Maverick Trevillian, shouting "six-seven!" with the gesture
  at a youth basketball game. Went viral, made him "the 67 kid" -- now over
  a million TikTok followers and his own merch. TREATMENT NOTE: he is a
  real, identifiable minor. This build does not depict his likeness in any
  AI-generated or real footage -- narration only, generic/abstract visuals.
- 15 Oct 2025: South Park season 28 premiere built a full subplot around it
  -- Cartman becomes unable to stop laughing whenever "six" or "seven" comes
  up, which convinces a fictional Peter-Thiel-styled billionaire character
  the kids have formed a satanic cult.
- Fortnite teased a Chapter 7 / South Park collab referencing it on 29 Nov
  2025 (emote released mid-Dec); a microwave prop on a character's mech suit
  is set to "67 seconds."
- Documented on-camera use: NBA highlights and college basketball games
  nationwide, NFL touchdown celebrations, WNBA press conferences, referees
  ("ref does 67" clips), Premier League footballer Michail Antonio, and (per
  AOL/wire coverage) a member of the US House of Representatives doing the
  gesture while presiding over a session.
- Dictionary.com named "67" (pronounced "six-seven," never "sixty-seven")
  its official 2025 Word of the Year, describing it as deliberately
  nonsensical "brainrot" slang; per their own analysis, usage in digital
  media was roughly 6x higher in October 2025 alone than across all of 2024.
