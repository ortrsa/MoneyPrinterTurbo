SOURCES CHECKED:
- JMBullion / History Facts / US Mint / ABC News: the US penny cost ~3.69
  cents to produce in 2025 (more than 3x face value); the US Mint ended
  penny production on 2026-11-12 2025 -- confirmed via US Mint's own FAQ
  page and multiple news outlets, not a single source. Scripted in past
  tense since production has actually ended.
- CNBC / Tripbase / Remitly: the Kuwaiti dinar is the most valuable
  currency unit in circulation, worth a little over 3 USD per note.
- GreekReporter / LBMA / American Numismatic Association: the first coins
  were minted in Lydia (western Anatolia, modern Turkey) c. 600 BC under
  King Alyattes, struck from electrum (a natural gold-silver alloy).
- Wikipedia / Tripbase: the Zimbabwean 100-trillion-dollar note, printed
  during 2008 hyperinflation and abandoned in 2009, now sells to
  collectors for well over 100 real USD -- genuinely more valuable as a
  collectible than it ever was as functional currency.
- Bank of Canada Museum / NPR / Atlas Obscura / BEYONDbones (multiple
  independent sources, not a single blog): Yap Island rai stones --
  giant carved limestone discs, some up to ~12 feet across and several
  tons; value is tracked by collective oral history rather than physical
  possession, so ownership can change hands without the stone ever
  moving. The specific sunk-stone story (a rai lost overboard in a storm
  during transport, still recognised as valid currency by common
  agreement since everyone knew it existed and who owned it) is
  corroborated across NPR, Atlas Obscura, and the Bank of Canada Museum
  as the same well-documented island account, not a single unverifiable
  tall tale.
- CHECKED AND REJECTED: the widely-repeated claim that more Monopoly
  money is printed annually than real US currency. Current data (Federal
  Reserve 2024 figures: $180-204B in new US currency vs. Hasbro's ~$30B
  in Monopoly money) shows this is false today, regardless of how often
  it's repeated online -- not used in the script.

VERIFIED FACTS:
- US penny: cost ~3.69 cents to make (2025), production ended Nov 2025.
- Kuwaiti dinar: most valuable circulating currency unit, ~3 USD.
- First coins: Lydia, c. 600 BC, electrum alloy.
- Zimbabwe 100-trillion note: worth more as a collectible than as money.
- Yap rai stones: huge, sometimes literally too heavy to move; ownership
