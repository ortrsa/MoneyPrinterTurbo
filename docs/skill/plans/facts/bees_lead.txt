BEES — ranked countdown (A/B Arm A), episode 45. Sourced and fact-checked 2026-08-16.

Format: countdown #6 -> #1, deliberately arguable #1, outro invites disagreement.
All six independently verified via web search; hedges noted per fact.

#6  Honeybees are not native to the Americas.
    The Virginia Company shipped hives to Jamestown in a fleet that arrived April 1622.
    ~4,000 native bee species already existed in North America, none of them honey producers.
    Native Americans reportedly called the European honeybee "the white man's fly."
    HEDGE: the "white man's fly" line is widely repeated in secondary sources; kept because
    it is attributed, not stated as the bees' own history. 231 years from east coast to west.
    Sources: thebfarm.com, National Honey Board, LA County Beekeepers Assoc.

#5  A single worker bee makes about 1/12 of a teaspoon of honey in her entire life.
    Worker lifespan ~5-6 weeks in spring/summer. A 16oz jar = 1,100+ bees' foraging.
    HEDGE (IMPORTANT): this is an educated average, NOT a measurement — there is no way to
    measure one bee's output, and it shifts with weather, nectar, season, colony. Script says
    "about", never a precise claim.
    Sources: Local Hive Honey, Carolina Honeybees, Apiary Project.

#4  Honey does not spoil.
    Honey recovered from sealed Egyptian tombs 3,000+ years old has not spoiled. Mechanism is
    real chemistry: very low water content, low pH (acidic), and hydrogen peroxide produced by
    a bee enzyme — together these make microbial growth almost impossible.
    HEDGE (IMPORTANT): the popular "archaeologists tasted it and it was still sweet" detail is
    only *reportedly* sourced and is DELIBERATELY LEFT OUT. Script anchors on the chemistry and
    on the honey being unspoiled, which is well supported. Same discipline as ep44's Wojtek
    "never dropped a shell" omission.
    Sources: Dallas Express, SpaceDaily, History Facts, STEMfinity.

#3  Bees can be trained to recognise individual human faces.
    >80% accuracy discriminating a target face from a similar distractor, and they hold the
    recognition across viewpoints. Brain ~1mm across, ~1 million neurons.
    They use CONFIGURAL PROCESSING — identity from the spatial arrangement of features — the
    same strategy the human visual system uses, evidenced by the fact that rotating stimuli
    180 degrees collapses their performance, exactly as face inversion impairs humans.
    Sources: J. Exp. Biol. 2005 (208:4709) and 2010 (213:593), PubMed 16326952 / 20118310,
    ScienceDaily.

#2  The waggle dance encodes both direction and distance.
    Angle of the waggle run relative to vertical = bearing to the food relative to the sun;
    duration of the waggle = distance. Decoded by Karl von Frisch, Nobel Prize 1973.
    Textbook-solid, no hedge needed.

#1  (ARGUABLE PAYOFF) Honeybees are not endangered — they are livestock, and keeping more of
    them may be actively harming the wild bees that are actually in trouble.
    Cambridge researchers argue honeybees should be thought of as livestock, not wildlife, and
    that conflating managed-colony die-offs with the wild-pollinator conservation crisis
    confuses the public. Beekeeping is an extractive activity: it removes nectar and pollen
    that wild species need. 78% of reviewed studies found exploitative competition from honey
    bees toward wild pollinators. High-density beekeeping measured at -55% wild bee occurrence
    and -50% nectar foraging success. Promoting hives inside protected areas likely worsens
    wild pollinator loss.
    WHY THIS IS A LEGITIMATE ARGUABLE #1: this is a real, live disagreement between beekeepers
    and bee conservationists with published evidence on both sides — not a manufactured
    controversy. Directly contradicts the "save the bees" message most viewers have absorbed,
    which is exactly the disagreement the countdown outro asks for. Same class of fit as ep41's
    Guinness largest-organism dispute.
    Sources: University of Cambridge research news, Nature Sci Reports (s41598-018-27591-y),
    ScienceDirect (S1470160X20306865, S221204162300030X), PMC6006304, PMC8081269.
