FACT 1 — largest desert
"Antarctica is the largest desert on Earth, bigger than the Sahara; deserts
are defined by rainfall."
Verified: ~14.2M km2 vs Sahara ~9.2M km2. A desert is any region under
250mm/yr precipitation regardless of temperature.
Sources: Discover Wildlife; Mappr; Aurora Expeditions; Poseidon Expeditions.

*** CORRECTION APPLIED — DO NOT RESTORE THE COMMON VERSION OF THIS FACT ***
The widely repeated claim "Antarctica is drier than the Sahara" is NOT true
as a continent-wide average: Antarctica averages ~166mm/yr precipitation vs
the Sahara's ~76mm/yr. Only Antarctica's INTERIOR (<50mm/yr) beats the
Sahara. The script therefore claims LARGEST, not DRIEST, which is
unambiguously correct. Same class of error as the ep36 Egypt toothpaste
attribution — a true-sounding claim that collapses on checking.
Also avoided: "no rain for 14 million years" / "2 million years" figures for
the Dry Valleys, which vary wildly between popular sources.

FACT 2 — it was once a rainforest
"Ninety million years ago a temperate rainforest grew there, less than a
thousand kilometres from the South Pole."
Verified: 2020 study on a sediment core from the seafloor off West
Antarctica found preserved roots, pollen and spores from a mid-Cretaceous
(92-83 Mya) temperate rainforest within ~900km of the South Pole. Mean
annual temp ~12-13C, summers up to 20-25C, despite a four-month polar night.
Sources: Nature; Imperial College London; Science News; Live Science; CBS.

FACT 3 — Blood Falls
"Blood Falls runs rust-red because ancient iron-rich brine oxidizes the
moment it hits the air."
Verified: brine flows from beneath Taylor Glacier into Lake Bonney; iron in
the brine oxidizes on contact with oxygen, turning it rust red. The brine is
thought to be ancient trapped seawater, kept liquid by its salt content.
Sources: National Geographic; Smithsonian Magazine; Wikipedia; Polar Journal.
NOTE: 2023 work refined the mechanism (iron-rich nanospheres rather than
simple oxide minerals). The script's phrasing — iron-rich brine oxidizing on
contact with air — remains correct under the refined account, so no hedge is
needed, but do not add specific mineral claims.

FACT 4 / PAYOFF — Mount Erebus
"Mount Erebus, the world's southernmost active volcano, has churned a lava
lake since at least 1972."
Verified: 3,794m, on Ross Island, ~35km from McMurdo Station. Persistent
convecting phonolite lava lake, continuously active since at least 1972 —
one of only a handful of long-lived lava lakes on Earth.
Sources: NASA Earth Observatory; Britannica; VolcanoDiscovery; Eos.
