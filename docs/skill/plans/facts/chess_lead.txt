VERIFIED FACTS:
- Chess boxing is a real hybrid sport (est. 2003, World Chess Boxing
  Organisation): alternating rounds of speed chess and boxing, win by
  checkmate, knockout, or (on a chess draw) judges' decision.
- The queen was originally one of the weakest pieces, moving only one
  square diagonally. The "Great Chess Reform" in late-15th-century Spain
  gave her the rook's and bishop's combined range, making her the most
  powerful piece almost overnight. Historians link the timing to powerful
  female monarchs of the era (e.g. Isabella I of Castile), though that
  causal link is cultural context, not proven causation -- scripted as the
  piece's actual rule change, which is the solid, sourced part.
- Nikolic-Arsovic (Belgrade, 1989) is the longest tournament chess game on
  record: 269 moves, 20 hours 15 minutes, ended in a draw.
- Deep Blue (IBM) beat Garry Kasparov on May 11, 1997, becoming the first
  computer to defeat a reigning world champion in a match under standard
  tournament time controls (final score 3.5-2.5 across six games).
- Chess is not a solved game -- unlike checkers (solved 2007, proven a
  forced draw under perfect play), nobody knows whether perfect play in
  chess is a win for White, a win for Black, or a draw. The complexity
  (roughly 35 legal moves per turn, 100+ move games) makes brute-force
  solving infeasible with any foreseeable computing power.
- The Shannon number (~10^120) estimates the number of possible chess
  games. The observable universe contains an estimated ~10^80 atoms. The
  gap is roughly 10^40 -- chess's game tree is that many times larger than
  the atom count of everything we can see.
