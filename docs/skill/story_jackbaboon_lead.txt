3	THE FACTS (independently verified):
	- James Edwin Wide, a guard for the Cape Government Railways in South Africa, earned the nickname "Jumper" for jumping on and off moving trains -- until he mistimed a jump, fell under a train, and lost both legs.
	- He returned to work at Uitenhage railway station (near Port Elizabeth) using two homemade wooden legs.
	- He bought a chacma baboon he saw skillfully driving an oxcart at a local market, named him Jack, and kept him as a companion/assistant, using him to help push his trolley between his cottage and the signal cabin.
	- Jack watched Wide operate the signal levers and picked up the pattern himself, eventually able to work the levers/switches without Wide's direct guidance.
	- After a passenger complained to the railway authorities about seeing a baboon operating signals, superintendent George B. Howe investigated and tested Jack directly rather than firing Wide -- Howe reported Jack knew the signal whistle codes and every lever perfectly.
	- The railway formally employed Jack, giving him an official employee number and a small wage. Multiple sources report he worked for about nine years and, per the historical record as commonly cited, never made a single signaling error in that time.
	Sources: Amusing Planet ("Jack The Baboon Signalman"), Mental Floss ("Signalman Jack: The Baboon Who Worked for the Railroad -- and Never Made a Mistake"), A-Z Animals, 2oceansvibe News.
