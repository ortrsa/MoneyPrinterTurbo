Topic lead (fact-checked independently before use; widely reported by NYT, BBC, and
The Guardian in April 2016 -- treat those as the corroborating sources, not a single
aquarium press release; do not copy any outlet's wording, only the underlying facts):

Inky was a common New Zealand octopus living at the National Aquarium of New Zealand
in Napier. He had arrived at the aquarium some years earlier after being caught, injured,
in a crayfish pot -- he was nursed back to health there and became a resident display
animal, not a wild-caught show animal kept against his health.

One night in 2016, a staff member doing maintenance on his tank apparently left the lid
not fully secured. Octopuses have no bones except for their hard beak, which is roughly
the size of a parrot's beak -- their entire body can pour through any gap wider than that
beak. Inky used this to climb out of his tank enclosure.

He then crossed the aquarium floor -- octopuses can survive brief stretches out of water
as long as their gills stay damp -- and found a drainpipe that led out of the building.
That pipe ran roughly 50 metres and emptied directly into Hawke's Bay, the ocean.

Staff arriving the next morning found the lid of his tank ajar and a trail of dry
suction-cup marks on the floor leading straight to the drain opening. There was no blood
and no sign of distress -- just the trail. Inky was never seen or recovered afterward,
and the aquarium's manager publicly said they assumed he made it back to the sea, since
that drain led nowhere else.

Narrative shape for the script: this is not a chaotic accident, it is a slow, deliberate
escape by an animal that quietly worked out the one flaw in the room and took it while
no one was looking. The hook should open on the state found the next morning (an empty
tank, a trail with no explanation) rather than stating "an octopus escaped" outright --
let the reveal explain what happened and why it was possible (boneless body, beak-sized
gap limit). The reveal should close the loop on the opening image: the trail was the
only evidence he ever existed there.
