FACT-CHECK LEAD — episode 81 (FACTS): honeybees

VERIFIED FACTS (WebSearch, 2026-08-31; beekeeping/education sources incl.
Montgomery County Beekeepers, BuzzAboutBees, Idaho Honey Industry
Association, eeBoo, plus waggle-dance research literature):
1. The waggle dance: honeybees communicate the DISTANCE to a food source
   and its DIRECTION relative to the sun (and quality) through a
   figure-eight "waggle" dance performed on the comb. Decoded by Karl von
   Frisch, who shared the 1973 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for
   it.
2. Honeybees see ultraviolet light, which helps them pick out
   nectar-rich flowers (many flowers carry UV patterns that act as
   landing guides invisible to us). They CANNOT see red.
3. A single worker bee produces only about one twelfth of a teaspoon of
   honey in her entire life. Producing one pound of honey takes roughly 2
   million flower visits and about 55,000 miles of flying. Worker
   lifespan is around 4-6 weeks in season.

HEDGE DISCIPLINE: the 1/12-teaspoon, 2-million-flowers and 55,000-miles
figures are long-standing, widely-repeated beekeeping-literature
estimates rather than a single precise measurement -- say "about" on each
and don't stack all three in one breath. The waggle dance and UV vision
are settled science and can be stated flatly.

STANCE / FRAMING: lead with the least-expected framing -- bees aren't
just foraging, they are actively giving each other navigational
instructions. Reframes a familiar animal as a communicating one.

LOOP DESIGN: open on "bees don't just find flowers - they give each other
directions"; close by asking the viewer which fact they're passing on
today, so the ending echoes the opening idea of information being handed
between individuals.
