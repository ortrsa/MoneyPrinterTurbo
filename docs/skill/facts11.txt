Frogs can breathe partly through their skin, absorbing oxygen directly from water, which is why many frogs can stay almost motionless floating at the surface for long stretches without needing to surface for air the way other animals do.
Frogs have a third eyelid called a nictitating membrane, a thin translucent layer that closes sideways across the eye, letting the frog keep watching prey or danger even while the eye stays protected and moist underwater.
When a frog swallows food, it blinks and pulls its own eyeballs down into its skull; the eyeballs push against the roof of its mouth from inside and physically shove the food down its throat.
