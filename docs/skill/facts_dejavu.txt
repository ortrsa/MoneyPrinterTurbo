Deja vu is French for "already seen," and most people experience it at least once in their life.
It's most common between ages 15 and 25 - then it actually gets rarer as you get older, which is the opposite of how most memory quirks work.
The term was first used back in 1876, by French philosopher Emile Boirac.
People with temporal lobe epilepsy get deja vu far more often - and doctors can actually trigger that exact feeling by electrically stimulating that part of the brain.
The leading theory: your brain's "this feels familiar" signal misfires without an actual matching memory behind it - though nobody has proven that's the full explanation yet.
In 2025, researchers tested whether people could actually predict what happens next during deja vu. They couldn't, at all - that eerie feeling of knowing what's coming is an illusion.
