VERIFIED FACTS (WebSearch, 2026-09-01; Britannica, Wikipedia (Clever Hans /
Oskar Pfungst), Lancaster Farming, Project MUSE):
- Clever Hans was a horse in early-1900s Germany, owned by Wilhelm von
  Osten, who appeared to solve arithmetic and other intellectual tasks,
  answering by tapping his hoof.
- A formal commission investigated (1904) and found no deliberate
  trickery -- the owner genuinely believed the horse was doing the maths.
- In 1907 Oskar Pfungst, working as a volunteer assistant in Carl
  Stumpf's laboratory in Berlin, ran the decisive investigation. He
  varied the questioner, sometimes letting them know the answer and
  sometimes deliberately keeping them unaware, and changed how questions
  were presented and the setting.
- Result: Hans was not performing intellectual tasks. He was responding
  to involuntary cues in the questioner's body language -- tapping until
  a subtle, unconscious "tell" signalled the answer had been reached.
  The trainer was entirely unaware he was producing those cues.
- This is the origin of the "Clever Hans effect", which remains important
  to the observer-expectancy effect and to the design of animal-cognition
  studies to this day.

SOURCES: Britannica ("Clever Hans | Story, Effect, & Facts"), Wikipedia
(Clever Hans; Oskar Pfungst), Lancaster Farming (Horses & History),
Project MUSE review.

STANCE TAKEN: the debunk is the better story. Hans wasn't a fraud and
neither was his owner -- and a horse that reads human micro-expressions
