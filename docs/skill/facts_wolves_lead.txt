VERIFIED FACTS (WebSearch, 2026-09-01; Scientific American, Science Arena
interview with L. David Mech, A-Z Animals, Turpentine Creek Wildlife
Refuge, Lobos of the Southwest):
1. The "alpha/beta" wolf hierarchy came from mid-20th-century studies of
   CAPTIVE wolves -- unrelated adult animals with no shared kinship
   thrown together. A dominance hierarchy does arise in that situation,
   but it is not how wolves behave when left to themselves.
2. A wild wolf pack is a family: a breeding male, a breeding female and
   their offspring from the past two or three years that haven't yet
   dispersed -- roughly six to ten animals. The "alpha male" and "alpha
   female" are simply the mother and father.
3. L. David Mech helped popularize the alpha language in his influential
   1970 book "The Wolf: Ecology and Behavior of an Endangered Species".
   He then spent 13 summers observing free-living packs on Ellesmere
   Island in the Canadian Arctic, and in 1999 published "Alpha Status,
   Dominance, and Division of Labor in Wolf Packs", arguing the term does
   not apply to wild wolves -- they don't dispute leadership, they just
   form families. He has spent decades trying to get the idea retracted.

SOURCES: Scientific American ("Is the Alpha Wolf Idea a Myth?"), Science
Arena (interview with Mech on scientific self-correction), A-Z Animals,
Turpentine Creek Wildlife Refuge, Lobos of the Southwest.
