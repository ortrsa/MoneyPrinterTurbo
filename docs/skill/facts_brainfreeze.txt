Brain freeze usually strikes when you eat or drink something cold too fast - slowing down is basically the only real prevention.
The fastest cure: press your tongue against the roof of your mouth, or sip something warm, to warm the area back up.
Cold hits the roof of your mouth, blood vessels there rapidly constrict, then flood back open - that sudden shift triggers the pain.
People who get migraines are more likely to get brain freeze too, probably because their pain nerves are already extra sensitive.
Brain freeze has an actual medical name: sphenopalatine ganglioneuralgia. Doctors really do call it that.
In 2012, Harvard researchers scanned brains in real time and found the pain lines up with one blood vessel rapidly expanding, then shrinking.
