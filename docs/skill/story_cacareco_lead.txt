1	True story: Cacareco the rhinoceros, São Paulo city council election, October 1959. ANIMAL category (per the 2026-08-18 topic-drift finding).

2	Setup: October 1959, São Paulo, Brazil municipal elections. Voters were fed up with political corruption, inflation, and food shortages under the state government of Adhemar de Barros. A local journalist (named in some sources as Itaboraí Martins), unimpressed with the field of human candidates, proposed a write-in protest candidate: Cacareco, a five-year-old female rhinoceros living at the São Paulo Zoo. Ballots were printed and distributed (student involvement is mentioned in some accounts as the distribution mechanism).

3	The result: Cacareco won roughly 100,000 votes -- about 15% of all votes cast in that race -- more than any of the other 540 candidates running for 45 city council seats. This figure (100,000 votes / 15%) is consistent across every source checked (Wikipedia, TIME archive, Daily Beast, hoaxes.org, Grokipedia, multiple secondary sources), no dispute found.

4	The aftermath: election officials had no legal mechanism to seat a non-human candidate, so all of Cacareco's votes were nullified and the city council race was rerun the following week.

5	The legacy: "voto Cacareco" (the Cacareco vote) became -- and remains -- Brazilian political slang for a protest vote against unsatisfying candidates, still in use in Brazilian political discourse today (per Wiktionary, multiple secondary sources).
