3	THE FACTS (independently verified):
	- June 23, 1868: Christopher Latham Sholes, with Carlos Glidden and Samuel W. Soule, patented one of the first practical typewriters in Milwaukee.
	- The first keyboard layout was alphabetical (A, B, C...) and jammed constantly -- frequently-typed adjacent letters caused the mechanical typebars to strike each other and lock up.
	- Sholes rearranged the keys specifically to separate commonly-paired letters from each other, reducing typebar clashes -- this became the QWERTY layout, first documented in US Patent No. 207,559 (Aug 27, 1878).
	- In 1873 Sholes sold the patent rights to E. Remington and Sons for $12,000; Remington marketed it as the "Remington Typewriter" and QWERTY spread from there.
	- QWERTY persisted after typebars became obsolete (and later, digital keyboards removed the jamming problem entirely) purely through path dependence -- everyone already knew it, every machine shipped with it, typing schools taught it for a century.
	Sources: Smithsonian Magazine ("The QWERTY Keyboard Will Never Die"), Britannica (Christopher Latham Sholes), National Inventors Hall of Fame, EBSCO Research Starters, UW-Madison wi101.wisc.edu ("Origins of the QWERTY Keyboard" + the specific myth-vs-telegraph-history page), History Facts, Built by Buit.
