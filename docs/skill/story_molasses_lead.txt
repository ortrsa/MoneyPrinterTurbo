FACT-CHECK LEAD — episode 78 (STORY): The Great Molasses Flood, Boston 1919

TOPIC: The Great Molasses Flood, Boston's North End, January 15, 1919.
Angle: "this sounds like a joke, it isn't" - a wave of molasses actually
killed people. Underrated-disaster stance, no myth-correction needed here
(the story itself is the twist), matches the channel's "the real story is
weirder/more serious than it sounds" pattern.

VERIFIED FACTS (WebSearch, 2026-08-31; Britannica, History.com, Wikipedia,
Massachusetts Secretary of State's office):
- January 15, 1919: a huge steel storage tank on Commercial Street in
  Boston's North End, owned by Purity Distilling Company (a subsidiary of
  United States Industrial Alcohol), suddenly burst.
- It released about 2 million gallons of molasses.
- Temperatures had risen rapidly (above 40°F) after a cold snap, and a
  fresh warm load of molasses had just been added to the tank -
  investigators later concluded thermal stress from that swing, combined
  with a tank that was not strong enough for the load, caused the burst.
- The molasses surged out as a wave 15 to 40 feet high and about 160 feet
  wide, moving at roughly 35 miles per hour - fast enough to outrun
  people on foot.
- It destroyed several city blocks: buildings were knocked off their
  foundations, elevated train supports were bent, and people, horses, and
  wagons were engulfed.
- 21 people were killed (many by suffocation in the syrup) and about 150
  were injured.
- A 6-year legal investigation followed - one of the largest class-action
  suits in Massachusetts at the time (~3,000 witnesses, ~45,000 pages of
  testimony) - and found United States Industrial Alcohol at fault: the
  tank had not been built strong enough for what it held.

SOURCES: Britannica, History.com (two separate articles), Wikipedia,
Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth (sec.state.ma.us), New England
Historical Society.

STANCE TAKEN: no myth to correct - the "twist" is that this isn't an urban
legend or exaggeration, it's a real, thoroughly documented disaster that
still gets treated like a punchline because "molasses" sounds silly. Lean
into that: "everyone laughs at this one until they hear the death count."

HEDGE DISCIPLINE: the exact CAUSE (thermal expansion vs. structural
failure) is what the 6-year investigation concluded, not eyewitness fact -
phrase as "investigators found" / "the inquiry concluded," not flat
certainty. Death toll (21) and speed/height of the wave are well-sourced
across multiple independent outlets - safe to state directly.

LOOP DESIGN: open on the sudden collapse / wave bearing down (visual
pattern interrupt + curiosity gap: "this is why molasses used to be
illegal to store like this"); close by circling back to how absurd the
premise still sounds even knowing it's true.
