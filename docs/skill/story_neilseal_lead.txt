1	True story: Neil the Seal, Tasmania -- built 2026-08-20 on direct owner request for a TRENDING topic. ANIMAL category (the channel's best-performing category per the 2026-08-18 topic-drift finding).

2	WHY THIS TOPIC: owner asked for something (1) interesting, (2) eye-catching, (3) from today's top Google trends that fits this channel and audience. Google's actual trending list for 2026-08-19/20 was dominated by sports fixtures, celebrity news and product recalls -- none of which fit an evergreen absurd-but-true format. Searched instead for currently-viral ANIMAL stories and found three live candidates: Punch the macaque (Japan), Jimothy the raccoon (Seattle), and Neil the Seal (Tasmania). Selected Neil; see item 7 for why the others were rejected.

3	THE FACTS (all independently verified):
	- Neil is a southern elephant seal, born on the Tasman Peninsula in 2020, so ~5 years old. Weight consistently reported at roughly one tonne / ~2,000 lb.
	- He hauls out onto Tasmanian coastal towns roughly twice a year -- a mid-year haul-out and again to moult. Most recently spotted at Seven Mile Beach on 2026-06-27, and returned to sea 2026-07-08 (confirmed by Tasmania's Department of Natural Resources and Environment via their own Facebook update, 2026-07-09).
	- Documented behaviour: blocking roads, ramming/damaging parked cars, overturning road posts, sleeping in residential streets. Sources: ABC News (Australia), CNN, Scientific American, Fox Weather, The Mirror, NPR.
	- Following: more than 1.5 million social media fans; a dedicated public Facebook group with over 50,000 members.
	- Official distance rules: the Tasmanian NRE asks the public to stay at least 20 metres away from Neil, and to keep dogs leashed and at least 50 metres away.

4	THE TWIST / PAYOFF (the reason this story is worth telling, and independently verified): Tasmanian officials' stated concern is NOT property damage -- it is the crowds. NRE has explicitly raised the risk of "loving him to death," and has publicly cited the precedent of Freya the walrus in Norway. Freya: a ~1,300-pound walrus who drew crowds in Oslo Fjord in 2022; despite repeated official warnings, people swam with her, posed for photos, and some threw objects at her. On 2022-08-14 Norwegian authorities euthanised her, citing public safety -- explicitly because the public would not keep its distance, not because she had injured anyone. Relocation was considered and rejected as unviable. Sources for Freya: CNN, NBC News, Washington Post, CBS News, UPI, PBS NewsHour, TODAY.

5	HEDGE APPLIED: the script does NOT claim Neil is going to be euthanised or that officials have threatened it -- that would be overreach. It states what is actually sourced: officials are worried about the crowds rather than the cars, they enforce distance rules, and Freya is the documented precedent that officials themselves invoke. The causal link is presented as the officials' stated concern, not as a prediction.

6	SENSITIVE-CONTENT NOTE: no named living humans are depicted or characterised. Neil is a wild animal, not a pet, and the episode does not encourage approaching him -- if anything the payoff is the opposite, which is the honest read of the sourcing.

7	REJECTED CANDIDATES (recorded so a later session does not re-litigate):
	- PUNCH THE MACAQUE (Ichikawa City Zoo, Japan; ~40M views in Feb 2026 alone; IKEA orangutan plush sold out worldwide). Rejected on two grounds: (a) it is a CONTESTED, actively disputed story -- the zoo has had to publicly rebut the widely-shared "Punch is being bullied" framing, and an animal-rights group has separately criticised his treatment, so the popular version of the story is exactly the version that is wrong; (b) it is about one specific, identifiable, currently-living zoo animal, so there is no legitimate footage available and generating AI footage of him would be fabricating imagery of a real individual in an active welfare dispute. Same failure class as the Cher Ami rejection (2026-08-20): the disputed part is the story's core, not an embellishment.
	- JIMOTHY THE RACCOON (Seattle): a viral raccoon possibly affected by short spine syndrome. Rejected -- the medical claim is speculative in the sourcing, and building a comedy beat on an animal's suspected deformity is a bad fit for this channel's tone.
	- Neil, by contrast, is documented by government agencies and mainstream outlets, the absurd part IS the verified part, and the payoff (Freya) is a separate, fully documented historical event.

8	FOOTAGE APPROACH (per the owner's explicit caution that excessive AI generation can itself provoke a negative reaction): majority REAL Pexels footage -- real elephant seals, real coastline, real roads. AI generation used only for the specific composite image Pexels genuinely cannot supply (a one-tonne seal lying across a suburban road), which is also the mandatory STORY hook. Generic elephant-seal stock is used as a visual echo throughout and the script never claims any particular clip depicts Neil himself -- the same discipline used on ep47 (no Lady Gaga likeness) and ep51 (no claim that stock warships were the named ships).
