1	True story: the Cardiff Giant hoax, 1869.

2	Setup: On October 16, 1869, laborers digging a well on William "Stub" Newell's farm in Cardiff, New York unearthed a stone figure of a man, nearly 10.5 feet tall.

3	The plot: Newell's cousin, George Hull, had planned it for years. An atheist who'd argued with a revivalist preacher about Biblical giants, Hull had a sculptor carve a 10-foot, 3,000-pound giant from an 11-foot block of gypsum quarried near Fort Dodge, Iowa, then shipped it to Chicago to be sculpted, weathered with sulphuric acid, and given fake "pores" with needle-pointed hammers. It was buried on Newell's farm nearly a year before the staged "discovery."

4	The frenzy: Newell charged 25 cents admission, raised it to 50 cents within two days. Thousands came, many believing it was either a petrified man or proof of the giants described in Genesis 6:4. Skeptics (Andrew Dickson White, first president of Cornell; Yale paleontologist Othniel C. Marsh) called it an obvious fake almost immediately - gypsum wouldn't hold fresh tool marks after centuries buried.

5	The confession: On December 10, 1869, less than two months after the "discovery," George Hull confessed to the press that the whole thing was a hoax.

6	The payoff (the twist): P.T. Barnum offered $50,000 to buy the giant. When the owners refused, Barnum simply commissioned his own plaster replica and displayed IT as the real Cardiff Giant - and his fake of a fake drew bigger crowds than the original fake. When the giant's owners sued Barnum for exhibiting a fraudulent copy, the judge refused to hear the case unless they could first prove their OWN giant was genuine. They dropped the lawsuit. The real (fake) Cardiff Giant is now at the Farmers' Museum in Cooperstown, New York.
