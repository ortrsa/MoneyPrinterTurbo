3	THE FACTS (independently verified):
	- 1957, Hawthorne, New Jersey. Engineers Alfred W. Fielding and Marc Chavannes sealed two plastic shower curtains together, trapping small pockets of air, intending to create a textured 3D wallpaper. Sources: Smithsonian Magazine, Wikipedia ("Bubble wrap"), Sealed Air's own company history, Core77, todayifoundout.com.
	- The wallpaper idea did not sell. They then tried marketing it as greenhouse insulation -- that also failed to catch on.
	- In 1960, Fielding and Chavannes founded Sealed Air Corporation, still without a proven commercial use for the material.
	- A Sealed Air marketer, Frederick W. Bowers, recognized the material's potential as protective packaging and pitched it to IBM, who adopted it to ship their computers. This was the breakthrough that made bubble wrap a packaging staple. (Sources differ slightly on the exact year of the IBM pitch -- most cite "around 1959-1960"; the script does not state a specific year for the IBM adoption to avoid overstating precision the sources themselves do not agree on.)
	- Sealed Air Corporation is a real, still-operating packaging company (NYSE: SEE) that continues to manufacture Bubble Wrap-brand product today.
