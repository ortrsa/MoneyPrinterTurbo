THE CHAIN
1518: a large silver coin starts being minted in Joachimsthal, a silver-mining
valley in Bohemia (today Jachymov, Czech Republic). It was struck under a
Bohemian count, Hieronymus Schlick. Weight was about 27 grams of silver
(sources vary between roughly 27 and 29 grams -- say "about 27 grams" or just
"a big silver coin", do not state a precise figure as settled).

The name: "Joachimsthaler" = "of St. Joachim's Valley". **"Thal" (modern German
"Tal") simply means VALLEY.** This is the hinge of the whole story and the owner's
version did not spell it out -- keep it, it is what makes the payoff land.

The coin spread across the Holy Roman Empire. "Joachimsthaler" was a mouthful,
so it got shortened to just "thaler" -- literally "valley-er", the thing from the
valley. From there:
  - Dutch:       daalder
  - Scandinavia: daler
  - Spain:       dollar (via Habsburg rule linking Spain and the Holy Roman Empire)
                 -> the Spanish dollar / "piece of eight"

Spain used those coins throughout its overseas colonies, including the Americas.
The Spanish dollar circulated so widely there that when the United States was
founded it was already the money people actually used -- so the new country
adopted the name.

THE PAYOFF: the most powerful currency on Earth is named after a hole in the
ground in Bohemia, and the word literally just means "from the valley". The name
also predates American independence by more than 250 years.

DO NOT overclaim: the US dollar was adopted because the Spanish dollar was
already in common circulation, not because anyone in 1776 was thinking about
Bohemia. Keep the chain as etymology and circulation, not as a decision anyone
made.
