1	True story: the hijacking of Ethiopian Airlines Flight 702, February 17, 2014.

2	Setup: Flight 702 took off from Addis Ababa headed for Rome with 202 people aboard. Partway through the flight, once the captain left the cockpit for the toilet, the co-pilot - Hailemedhin Abera Tegegn - locked the cockpit door from the inside and took sole control of the plane.

3	The hijack: He radioed air traffic control and diverted the plane toward Geneva, Switzerland, declaring a fuel emergency to justify the course change. He was hijacking his own aircraft to seek asylum in Switzerland, saying he felt his life was in danger in Ethiopia. He circled Geneva airspace for a while, at one point flying low enough over the city that people on the ground could see the plane.

4	The twist: Fighter jets scrambled to intercept and identify the aircraft - but they weren't Swiss. Switzerland's air force only operates during business hours (roughly 8am-noon and 1:30-5pm on weekdays), and the hijacking happened before dawn on a Monday, outside that window. Swiss airbases were closed, so French and Italian fighter jets escorted the plane into Geneva airspace instead, under a standing arrangement with neighboring air forces.

5	The landing: The co-pilot landed the plane safely, taxied to a stop, then opened the cockpit window, tied a rope, and climbed down to the tarmac himself rather than let anyone approach the cockpit. He surrendered to police immediately, identifying himself as both the co-pilot and the hijacker. All 202 passengers and crew were evacuated unharmed.

6	The payoff: Switzerland later granted him asylum rather than prosecuting or extraditing him, after authorities found signs of paranoia in his mental state. No one was hurt in the entire incident - the only real casualty was Switzerland's air defense credibility, and the country moved to extend its air force's hours after the embarrassment.
