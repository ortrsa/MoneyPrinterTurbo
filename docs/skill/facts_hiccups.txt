American Charles Osborne hiccupped for sixty-eight straight years, from nineteen twenty-two to nineteen ninety - an estimated four hundred thirty million hiccups.
A hiccup is just your diaphragm spasming, then your vocal cords snapping shut - that's the actual "hic" sound you hear.
One leading theory: hiccups are a leftover reflex from our amphibian ancestors, who used the same nerve pathway to breathe through gills as tadpoles.
Some studies suggest a fetus spends up to two and a half percent of its day hiccupping, while still in the womb.
There's no scientifically proven cure for hiccups - holding your breath, drinking water upside down, all just attempts to stimulate the same nerve.
Chronic hiccups lasting more than forty-eight hours can sometimes signal something serious, like a brain tumor or stroke - worth seeing a doctor.
