STORY LEAD — Wojtek, the bear who was officially enlisted as a Polish soldier

VERIFIED FACTS (independently checked before scripting)

1. ORIGIN — 1942, near Hamadan, Iran. Soldiers of the 22nd Artillery Supply
   Company, Polish II Corps, obtained a Syrian brown bear cub from a local
   boy, trading supplies (accounts list canned beef, chocolate, a knife).
   Named him Wojtek.
   Sources: Wikipedia "Wojtek (bear)"; Britannica "Wojtek the Bear";
   Imperial War Museum / Eden Camp Modern History Museum.

2. THE TWIST — British regulations forbade pets and animal mascots aboard
   military transport ships bound for Italy. To get around this, the Polish
   II Corps formally ENLISTED him: he was given the rank of private, his own
   serial number, and a paybook, and was billeted with the troops.
   This is the hook. It is not folklore — it is the documented reason the
   enlistment happened at all.
   Sources: Britannica; Wikipedia; Edinburgh World Heritage; Historic
   Environment Scotland blog.

3. MONTE CASSINO, 1944 — during the battle Wojtek carried crates of
   artillery shells for the gun crews, imitating the men around him.
   Sources: Britannica; Forces News; Wikipedia.
   HEDGE APPLIED: popular retellings add "and never dropped a single one."
   That detail is not reliably sourced and reads as embellishment — it is
   deliberately NOT in the script. The crate-carrying itself is well attested.

4. AFTERMATH — promoted to corporal. The 22nd Company adopted an official
   emblem showing a bear carrying an artillery shell; it became the unit's
   badge.
   Sources: Wikipedia; Britannica; Aquila Polonica.

5. AFTER THE WAR — demobilised and sent to Edinburgh Zoo on 15 November
   1947. Died December 1963, aged 21. Statues now stand in Edinburgh's
   Princes Street Gardens and in Kraków.
   Sources: Britannica; Edinburgh World Heritage; HES blog.

CLAIMS DELIBERATELY AVOIDED
- Exact weight: commonly given as ~200 kg / 440 lb (Wikipedia), but popular
  articles inflate this to "500 pounds". Script says "six feet tall", which
  is consistently attested, and avoids a specific weight figure.
- "Smoked cigarettes / drank beer": widely repeated and probably true in
  some form, but it is anecdotal colour, not needed, and drinking/smoking
  framing is a poor fit for the audience. Left out.
- Wojtek did NOT fight or handle a weapon. The script says he carried
  ammunition crates, which is what the sources support.

  HOOK  — statement form (story convention, not a question): the Polish army
          had a soldier who was six feet tall and covered in fur.
  BEAT 1 — soldiers adopted a bear cub in Iran in 1942; he grew up in camp.
  BEAT 2 — the ship banned animals, so they enlisted him for real: rank,
          serial number, paybook.
  BEAT 3 / PAYOFF — at Monte Cassino he hauled crates of artillery shells,
