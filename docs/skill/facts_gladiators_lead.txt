FACT-CHECK LEAD — episode 79 (FACTS): Roman gladiators, myth-busting

VERIFIED FACTS (WebSearch, 2026-08-31; Through Eternity Tours, Oldest.org,
Flavor of Italy podcast, Walks of Italy):
1. The famous "thumbs down = death" hand signal is NOT an authentic
   ancient Roman gesture - it's a 19th-century artistic invention,
   popularized by French painter Jean-Leon Gerome's 1872 painting
   "Pollice Verso." What the real signal actually meant is disputed/not
   settled among historians.
2. Chemical/isotope analysis of skeletons from a gladiator cemetery in
   Ephesus found their diets were heavy in barley, beans, and other
   plant matter, with surprisingly little meat - nicknamed "barley men"
   by ancient sources, more like large trained athletes on a mostly
   vegetarian diet than the meat-heavy image usually assumed.
3. Owners had a financial incentive to keep gladiators alive - training
   them was expensive, so most fights were not fights to the death, and
   many successful gladiators fought (and survived) many bouts, becoming
   celebrities with fans and merchandise.

SOURCES: Through Eternity Tours ("Gladiators in the Roman Colosseum" and
"10 Surprising Facts About the Colosseum"), Oldest.org ("15 Fascinating
Facts About Roman Gladiators"), Flavor of Italy podcast ("Ten myths...
about gladiators").

STANCE / FRAMING: strong myth-busting stance available on fact #1 (the
thumbs-down gesture is Hollywood/19th-century art, not ancient Rome) -
state this directly, it's well-documented. Facts #2 and #3 reframe the
popular "brutal meat-eating killer" image as closer to "trained athlete
with a stage persona," which is itself a stance worth taking elegantly.

HEDGE DISCIPLINE: fact #1's ORIGIN (Gerome's painting, 19th century) is
solid and can be stated directly; what the real ancient gesture meant is
NOT settled -- do not claim to know the real meaning, just that the
famous version is a myth. Facts #2 and #3 are supported by modern
research/analysis -- state directly, no hedge needed.

LOOP DESIGN: open on the thumbs-down myth (curiosity gap: everyone knows
this gesture, most people don't know it's fake); close by tying back to
"everything you picture about gladiators" being more Hollywood than
history.
