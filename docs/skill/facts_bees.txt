Honeybees aren't native to the Americas. Colonists shipped hives to Virginia in 1622, and Native Americans called them the white man's fly.
A single worker bee makes only about a twelfth of a teaspoon of honey in her whole life. One jar is hundreds of lifetimes.
Honey doesn't spoil. Jars sealed in Egyptian tombs three thousand years ago still haven't, because honey is too dry and too acidic for microbes.
Bees can learn individual human faces. They use the same configural trick your brain does, with a brain about one millimetre across.
The waggle dance is a map. Its angle gives the food's direction relative to the sun, and the waggle's length gives the distance.
Honeybees aren't endangered. They're farmed livestock, and researchers argue that adding hives starves the wild native bees that actually do need saving.
