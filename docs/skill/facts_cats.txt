A cat's whiskers are rooted three times deeper than regular fur, wired into the nervous system to sense the dark.
Cats can see roughly six times better than humans in low light, thanks to a reflective layer behind the retina.
Cats can't taste sweetness at all - a genetic mutation broke their sugar receptor thousands of years ago, and it never came back.
A cat's purr sits around twenty-five to a hundred fifty hertz - frequencies some research links to bone and tissue healing.
Cats were never really domesticated. About ten thousand years ago, wildcats moved in on their own to hunt rodents near grain stores.
In a 1987 study, cats falling six-plus stories often had fewer injuries than ones falling from lower - they seem to relax after reaching terminal velocity.
