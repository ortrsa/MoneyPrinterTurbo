1	True story: Lady Gaga and the slow loris, February 2014.

2	Setup: During the three-day shoot for Lady Gaga's music video "G.U.Y." at Hearst Castle in California, an animal trainer brought several exotic animals to set, including a baby kangaroo, a goat, and a slow loris - a small, wide-eyed nocturnal primate from Southeast Asia.

3	The twist: the slow loris looks like a stuffed toy, but it's the only venomous primate on Earth. It has glands on the inside of its elbows that ooze an oily toxin; when the loris licks the gland and mixes that oil with its saliva, the bite becomes venomous - powerful enough to trigger fatal anaphylactic shock in a person, even though the loris never evolved the bite to kill humans.

4	The incident: during the shoot, the loris bit Lady Gaga. She reportedly laughed it off and was a good sport about it, and the loris was dropped from the video entirely - no animals ended up appearing in the final cut.

5	The bigger story: the bite made headlines and set off outrage from wildlife groups, because California state parks had already blocked the kangaroo and the goat from being used, and nobody could explain how the trainer had a slow loris in the first place - the species is protected everywhere it's found and all commercial trade in it is illegal. Slow lorises are targeted by the illegal pet trade specifically because they look cute in viral videos, and traffickers routinely rip out their teeth with pliers or wire cutters, with no anaesthetic, so the animals can't bite the humans handling them.

6	The payoff: a global pop star got bitten by one of the rarest, most trafficked animals on the planet, on a film set that wasn't supposed to have it there in the first place - and it's still the moment most people first heard that a "cute" primate could be venomous at all.
