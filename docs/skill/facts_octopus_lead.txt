FACT-CHECK LEAD — episode 77 (FACTS): octopus biology

VERIFIED FACTS (WebSearch, 2026-08-30; Smithsonian Magazine, Natural
History Museum UK, Shedd Aquarium):
1. Octopuses have three hearts. Two branchial hearts push blood through
   the gills to pick up oxygen; the third, systemic heart pumps that
   oxygenated blood to the rest of the body.
2. The systemic heart actually stops beating while the octopus swims -
   which is a real, sourced explanation for why octopuses favor crawling
   over swimming (swimming exhausts them).
3. Their blood is blue, not red - they use a copper-based protein called
   hemocyanin instead of iron-based hemoglobin, which works better than
   hemoglobin in cold, low-oxygen water (part of why octopuses can live in
   deep parts of the ocean).
4. The brain is donut-shaped, and each of the eight arms has its own
   cluster of neurons acting like a mini-brain - each arm can taste, touch,
   and move on its own, while the central brain still exercises top-down
   control.

SOURCES: Smithsonian Magazine ("Ten Wild Facts About Octopuses"), Natural
History Museum UK, Shedd Aquarium.
