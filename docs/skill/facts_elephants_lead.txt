2	ALL 6 FACTS INDEPENDENTLY WEB-VERIFIED:
	1. Gestation: elephant pregnancy lasts up to 22 months -- the longest of any mammal. Sources: Britannica, ArhFoundation, multiple.
	2. Trunk: an elephant's trunk contains over 40,000 individual muscles (no bones) -- allows fine motor control from birth. Source: Britannica/multiple elephant-fact compilations.
	3. Elephants can't jump -- not from size alone, but their leg bones, tendons and ankle joints aren't built for the "spring" a jump needs. HEDGE: elephants are NOT the only mammal with this limitation -- rhinos and hippos share it, for similar structural reasons. Source: LiveScience, Biology Insights.
	4. Mirror self-recognition: in a 2005 study, an elephant named Happy at the Bronx Zoo repeatedly used her trunk to touch a mark painted above her eye that she could only see in a mirror -- one of the clearest tests of self-awareness in a non-human animal, a trait otherwise confirmed mainly in great apes, dolphins, and magpies. Source: PubMed (Plotnik et al. 2006, PNAS), NBC News, Fox News.
	5. Matriarch memory: elephant matriarchs have been documented leading their herds to water sources not visited in 35-45 years, likely learned during a previous drought decades earlier. HEDGE: this is field-observation research (McComb et al.), described as "likely" / inferred from the matriarch's age and herd behavior, not a controlled lab-verified recall test. Source: Phangan Elephant Sanctuary summary of the published research; matches widely-cited matriarch-memory literature.
	6. Mourning: a 2019 study in Scientific Reports documented elephants showing decreased appetite, weight loss, and disrupted sleep after the death of a matriarch, and separately, researchers have documented elephants revisiting the sites where herd members died, sometimes years later. HEDGE: described as physiological/behavioral signs consistent with grief, not asserted as elephants definitely "feeling grief" the way humans do -- that interpretation gap is scripted carefully (researchers describe/document the behavior; the script does not claim to know the elephants' subjective experience).
