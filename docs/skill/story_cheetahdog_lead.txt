4	ORIGIN STORY (the actual narrative backbone -- this is the twist worth telling): 1976, Wildlife Safari, Winston, Oregon. Laurie Marker, curator of the park's cheetah-breeding program, was hand-raising a lonely cheetah cub named Khayam, born Dec 4 1976, with no other cheetah cubs to keep her company (cheetahs are natural littermates and don't do well totally alone). On a hunch, Marker paired Khayam with a Labrador-mix puppy named Shesho. It worked immediately -- the two became inseparable, and Shesho's calm, confident presence visibly relaxed Khayam. Khayam became the first cheetah "ambassador animal," and she and Marker (with Shesho) toured the US to raise cheetah-conservation awareness, and also traveled to what's now Namibia in 1977 for cheetah research. Laurie Marker later founded the Cheetah Conservation Fund (1990) and is still a leading cheetah researcher today -- named only via her own on-record, positive quotes about her life's work, no sensitivity issue (an established real, living conservation scientist speaking about her own well-documented career, same register as prior "real person spoke positively on record" precedents this session).
