In Sweden, many pizzerias serve "banana pizza" (sometimes called Pizza Africana or Curry pizza), topped with sliced banana, ham or chicken, and curry powder -- an unusual combination that has become a genuine, well-documented part of Swedish pizza culture, not a one-off novelty.
In Brazil, one of the most common pizza styles is "Pizza Portuguesa," topped with hard-boiled egg slices, green peas, onion, ham, and often olives -- found at the vast majority of Brazilian pizzerias.
In Japan, a popular pizza style is "potato mayo" (sometimes called meat mayo) pizza, topped with potato, corn, bacon, and Japanese mayonnaise drizzled in stripes over the top -- a mainstay at Japanese Pizza Hut and Domino's menus. Do not call this "mentai mayo" -- that name belongs to a different Japanese pizza topped with spicy cod roe (mentaiko), not potato.
In Finland, pizzerias sell the "Berlusconi pizza," topped with smoked reindeer meat and red onion. It was created in 2005 as a cheeky response after Italy's own prime minister, Silvio Berlusconi -- not Finland's leader -- publicly mocked Finnish cuisine during an EU debate. The script must make clear Berlusconi was Italy's prime minister, since "their prime minister" is ambiguous about which country he led.
In the Philippines, some pizza chains sell sweet mango pizza, topped with fresh mango slices and cheese, sometimes with a drizzle of chili sauce for contrast.
