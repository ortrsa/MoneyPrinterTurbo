3	THE FACTS (independently verified):
	- July 5, 1996, the Roslin Institute near Edinburgh, Scotland. Ian Wilmut and colleagues created Dolly, a Finn Dorset sheep, by fusing the nucleus of an adult mammary-gland cell (from one ewe) into an enucleated egg cell (from a different ewe, a Scottish Blackface) -- the first mammal cloned from an adult somatic cell.
	- It took 277 attempts; Dolly was the only lamb born alive.
	- The name: lab assistant John Bracken suggested "Dolly," on the spot, as a joke referencing Dolly Parton -- because the donor cell came from a mammary gland. Dolly Parton herself later said she was "flattered" when she learned about it.
	- Dolly's birth was announced publicly in February 1997 and became global news, sparking real public and scientific debate about the ethics and feasibility of human cloning.
	- In 1999, Nature published research suggesting Dolly's cells had unusually short telomeres, fueling a popular theory that cloned animals might age prematurely.
	- On February 14, 2003, Dolly was euthanized at age 6 due to progressive lung disease (later identified as ovine pulmonary adenocarcinoma, a retrovirus-caused sheep lung cancer) and arthritis.
	Sources: Britannica ("Dolly"), Wikipedia ("Dolly (sheep)"), Scientific American ("Dolly at 20"), Seattle Times/NBC News (death coverage), PBS NOVA, CBC.

4	HEDGE APPLIED (the actual twist of the story): Roslin Institute scientists stated they did NOT think Dolly's death was connected to her being a clone -- other, non-cloned sheep in the same indoor flock contracted the same lung disease, and indoor-housed sheep are generally more prone to it (Dolly was kept inside for security, given her fame). Her arthritis/weight may have been linked to her being fed treats as a celebrity animal. Later research on other cloned sheep (including four genomic copies of Dolly, tracked into old age) found no evidence of accelerated aging. The script does NOT assert "clones age faster" as fact -- it states the 1999 finding and the theory it fueled, then gives the scientists' own more nuanced explanation, exactly matching the sourcing.
