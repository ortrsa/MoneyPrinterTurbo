STORY LEAD — The moa: the only birds that had no wings at all
Supplied by the channel owner 2026-08-03. Independently fact-checked against
britannica.com/animal/moa, en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moa, teara.govt.nz/en/moa, and
the 2014 high-precision extinction chronology (ScienceDirect / PNAS).
ONE CORRECTION TO THE SUBMITTED VERSION — see below.

WHAT THEY WERE
Moa (order Dinornithiformes) were giant flightless birds found only in New
Zealand. The largest species stood over 3 metres tall with the neck raised and
weighed roughly 200-250 kg. Nine species are recognised.

THE REALLY GOOD FACT — this is the payoff, keep it:
Moa did not merely have small or hidden wings. They had NO WINGS AT ALL — not
even vestigial wing bones. They are the only known birds with no wing bones
whatsoever. Ostriches, emus, kiwis and penguins all still have wing bones;
moa lost them entirely. Millions of years with no ground predators to flee from
meant the entire structure was erased by evolution.

*** CORRECTION to the owner's submitted version: extinction was around
1400-1450, NOT the early 16th century. The most precise study dates the
extinction of all moa species to roughly 1380-1440. This CORRECTION MAKES THE
STORY BETTER, so do not soften it: Maori arrived about 1250-1300, which means
every moa species was gone within roughly 100-150 years of humans setting foot
on the islands. That is one of the fastest extinctions ever recorded. Do not say
"16th century" or "1500s". ***

Cause: hunting (meat, eggs, and skins/feathers for clothing) plus forest
burning. Importantly, the research found moa populations were large and healthy
right up until humans arrived -- they were NOT already in decline. So this is
not a species that was doomed anyway; it was thriving and then it was gone.

NARRATIVE SHAPE
This one has a natural two-stage structure. Open on the scale and the strangeness
(a bird taller than a person, with no wings at all -- not small wings, none), and
hold back WHY the wings vanished until it pays off the setup: nothing hunted them,
for millions of years, so the body simply stopped building the parts used for
escape. Then the turn: the one thing that could hunt them finally showed up, and
the same defencelessness that took millions of years to evolve killed the whole
order in about a century. Close on the speed -- a bird that had been safe for
millions of years lasted roughly 100-150 years once people landed.
