3	THE FACTS (independently verified):
	- September 10, 1945, Fruita, Colorado. Farmer Lloyd Olsen was beheading chickens for market. One young Wyandotte rooster, later named Mike, got back up and kept moving after the axe blow. Sources: Wikipedia, Snopes, Britannica, Scientific American, Sky HISTORY.
	- The axe took most of the head but missed the jugular vein, leaving one ear and most of the brain stem intact. Most of a chicken's basic motor/survival functions (breathing, balance) are controlled by the brain stem/cerebellum, located toward the back of the skull -- this is the actual mechanism Snopes/Britannica cite for why this is physically plausible, not just lucky.
	- Olsen kept Mike alive, feeding him with an eyedropper/syringe -- a mixture of milk and water, plus small grains of corn -- directly into his exposed esophagus.
	- Mike lived for approximately 18 months. He toured as "Miracle Mike," charged 25 cents admission, and was insured for $10,000 by sideshow promoter Hope Wade.
	- Guinness World Records credits Mike as the longest-surviving headless chicken on record.
	- Mike died in March 1947 (specifically March 17, per most sources) in a motel in Phoenix, Arizona -- roughly 18 months after the beheading, not because of it.
