	- Slender Man was created on June 10, 2009, in a Photoshop contest thread ("create paranormal images") on the Something Awful forums, by user Eric Knudsen, posting under the pseudonym "Victor Surge." Source: Wikipedia ("Slender Man"), corroborated by Newsweek, Rolling Stone, NBC News.
	- Knudsen's contest entries were two ordinary black-and-white photos of groups of children, into which he Photoshopped a tall, unnaturally thin, faceless figure in a black suit. He accompanied the images with a few lines of invented "witness" text describing child abductions, and coined the name "The Slender Man."
	- In a Know Your Meme interview Knudsen said he was inspired by shadow-people folklore, H.P. Lovecraft, Zack Parsons, Stephen King's "The Mist," and William S. Burroughs -- and when asked if he expected it to stay a one-thread joke between him and the forum, he answered "Absolutely."

None of this predecessor material is real; it was all created after June 2009 to retroactively fake a history.
	- The mythology exploded into found-footage web series (Marble Hornets, which coined the term "Proxy," later adopted across Creepypasta fandom), video games (Slender: The Eight Pages, 2012), and a 2018 feature film.

The victim crawled to a road and was found by a cyclist; she survived. Both attackers were later found not guilty by reason of mental illness/disease and committed to psychiatric institutions.
	- Eric Knudsen gave a statement to NBC News after the stabbing: "I am deeply saddened by the tragedy in Waukesha, Wisconsin. My thoughts are with the family of the victim..." (NBC News, "'Slender Man' Creator Speaks on Stabbing").
