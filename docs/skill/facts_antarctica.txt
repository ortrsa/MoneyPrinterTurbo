Antarctica is the largest desert on Earth, bigger than the Sahara, because a desert is defined by how little falls from the sky, not by heat.
The coldest temperature ever recorded on Earth was minus eighty nine point two Celsius, at Russia's Vostok Station in July nineteen eighty three.
Antarctica holds about ninety percent of all the ice on Earth. If it ever melted, global sea levels would rise roughly sixty metres.
Ninety million years ago a temperate rainforest grew there, less than a thousand kilometres from the South Pole, in a four month polar night.
Blood Falls pours rust red out of a glacier, because ancient iron rich brine trapped underneath oxidises the moment it touches the air.
Mount Erebus, the southernmost active volcano on Earth, has held a churning lava lake since at least nineteen seventy two, on the coldest continent.
